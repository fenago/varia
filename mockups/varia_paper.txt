VARIA: Benchmarking Frontier LLMs on Construct-Equivalent
Assessment Variant Generation
Dr. Ernesto Lee Miami Dade College elee@mdc.edu
Abstract
In plain terms: if we are going to let AI write personalized versions of tests for every student,
those versions have to be more than surface paraphrase — they have to look genuinely different on
the outside while still measuring the same underlying skill on the inside. This paper asks whether
today’s frontier AI models can actually do that, and offers a way to measure it.
AI-Integrated Authentic Assessment (AIAA) frameworks propose to replace surveillance-based proc-
toring with per-student task variation: each examinee receives a unique-yet-equivalent performance
task, making answer copying useless without invasive monitoring. The framework’s no-surveillance
integrity guarantee is conditional on a presumed but unverified capability — that large language
models can generate parallel variants of an assessment blueprint that are simultaneously surface-
distinct, construct-equivalent, rubric-applicable, and difficulty-matched. We introduce VARIA, a
benchmark that operationalises this presumption as four measurable properties of a generated
variant set. VARIA evaluates three frontier model families (Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7, OpenAI
GPT-5.5, Google Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview) across four prompting strategies (zero-shot, few-shot
with anchor examples, structured chain-of-thought, dimension-preserving constrained generation)
on three competency-based blueprints drawn from applied AI and data analytics, alongside two non-
frontier reference systems (a small open instruction-tuned model and GPT-2 small) and two targeted
ablations. A pilot of 600 generated variants across 60 condition cells reveals that frontier strategies
cluster tightly on a composite integrity score (joint J ∈ [0.81, 0.88]) while the non-frontier refer-
ences collapse to J ∈ [0.50, 0.55] — a forty-percentage-point gap that establishes the benchmark’s
discrimination. Within the frontier band, structured chain-of-thought and zero-shot prompting
maximise construct equivalence; dimension-preserving constrained generation and few-shot anchoring
maximise surface diversity; and the two regimes trade against each other on readability parity. We
release VARIA, recommend a generation protocol keyed to the deploying institution’s threat profile,
and pre-register a follow-up that will measure rubric stability and behavioural copy-resistance.
1. Introduction
The arrival of fluent generative language models has destabilised a core assumption of high-stakes
educational assessment: that what a student produces under examination conditions reflects what
that student can produce unaided. Conventional remedies — proctoring, lockdown browsers,
biometric surveillance — escalate intrusiveness without resolving the underlying problem, since
generative tools are now ambient on personal devices and in collaborative chats (Kasneci et al. 2023;
Yan et al. 2023). A growing literature on authentic assessment proposes a different solution: rather
than policing the examination room, redesign the assessment so that copying is structurally useless
(Salinas-Navarro et al. 2024; M. Brown et al. 2020). The proposal at the heart of this redesign is
variation-at-scale: each examinee receives an instance of a single competency blueprint, instantiated
with different surface details — domain, scenario, jargon, reading register — such that no two
students can productively share answers. The AIAA framework explicitly delegates the generation
of these variants to large language models (Chen et al. 2020; Gligorea et al. 2023), and recent

course-design proposals have begun to operationalise the idea in working classrooms (Mennella et al.
2024; Holmes et al. 2021).
The framework’s integrity claim, however, is conditional on an unverified capability. If LLMs
cannot generate variants that are surface-distinct yet construct-equivalent, rubric-applicable, and
difficulty-matched, then variation-at-scale is a fig leaf and the no-surveillance guarantee collapses.
Despite the speed with which AIAA-style proposals have been adopted in higher-education guidance
(Yan et al. 2023; Holmes et al. 2021), the capability presumption underlying them has not been
directly benchmarked. Existing work on automatic item generation in psychometrics is dominated
by template-based methods for multiple-choice items with closed-form answer keys, which sidestep
the rubric-applicability problem that defines open-response performance assessment (Panadero et
al. 2023; Fenn et al. 2020). LLM evaluation benchmarks measure model competence on fixed
items rather than the inverse problem of generating equivalent items (Chang et al. 2024; Guo
et al. 2023; Naveed et al. 2025). LLM-as-judge work has established scoring infrastructure but
not psychometric-equivalence protocols (Cohn et al. 2024; Fan et al. 2024). Paraphrase-diversity
research optimises surface variance against fixed references but does not constrain joint preservation
of an underlying competency construct (P. Liu et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2024).
We close this gap by introducing VARIA, a benchmark that formalises the AIAA integrity-via-
variation hypothesis as a four-property capability claim about LLMs and measures it directly.
VARIA defines a blueprint as a triple of competency, analytic rubric, and canonical solution,
and requires a generated variant set to simultaneously satisfy surface diversity sufficient to deter
copying (measured by pairwise embedding cosine and n-gram overlap), construct equivalence on the
underlying competency (measured by an LLM judge), rubric applicability across surface variation
(measured via canonical-solution readability dispersion as a pilot proxy for the full rubric-stability
protocol), and difficulty parity across surface features (measured by readability dispersion). We
instantiate VARIA on three competency-based blueprints from applied AI and data analytics — an
audit-style performance task, a stakeholder communication scenario, and an ethical risk analysis —
and evaluate three frontier model families against four prompting strategies, a small open instruction-
tuned reference (Llama 3.2 3B Instruct), a deep-floor reference (GPT-2 small, 124M parameters),
and two targeted ablations. Across 60 successful condition cells comprising 600 generated variants,
we find a clear capability-tier separation but a surprisingly tight within-frontier band: frontier
strategies span J ∈ [0.81, 0.88], while both non-frontier references fall below J = 0.55. Within the
frontier band, prompting strategy selects an operating point on the diversity-equivalence-readability
surface rather than producing a uniformly best protocol; structured chain-of-thought and zero-shot
occupy the construct-equivalence corner, dimension-preserving constrained generation occupies the
diversity corner at the cost of difficulty drift, and the GPT-2 reference exhibits exactly the failure
mode AIAA must avoid — near-zero construct equivalence and uncontrolled readability dispersion.
Our contributions are threefold. First, we offer a falsifiable formalisation of the AIAA integrity-via-
variation hypothesis as four measurable properties of a generated variant set, with explicit metrics,
thresholds, and a joint score. Second, we release the VARIA benchmark — three competency-based
blueprints, four prompting strategies, five complementary metrics, and an evaluation harness covering
Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google frontier models alongside a small open-instruct reference (Llama
3.2 3B Instruct) and a deep-floor reference (GPT-2 small). Third, we report an empirical pilot of
600 generated variants across 60 condition cells that charts the diversity-equivalence-readability
surface, demonstrates a forty-point capability-tier gap between frontier and non-frontier generators,
and identifies a production protocol keyed to deployment threat profile, while pre-registering the
rubric-stability and behavioural copy-resistance extensions that the AIAA argument ultimately

2. Related Work
VARIA sits at the intersection of three previously disjoint literatures: automatic item generation in
psychometrics, LLM applications in education, and controlled or diverse text generation in natural
language processing. We discuss each and position our contribution against the gaps that emerge
when these literatures are read together.
2.1 Automatic Item Generation and Parallel-Forms Equating
Classical automatic item generation grew out of cognitive psychometrics and targets the cost of
producing large item banks for standardised testing. The dominant paradigm uses cognitive models
or templates specifying item radicals (the stable construct content) and incidentals (the surface
variation), generating item families whose members are presumed equivalent by construction . This
template-driven approach has succeeded for closed-form item types — multiple choice, fill-in-the-
blank, short answer with a known key — because equivalence can be guaranteed at the symbolic
level. Parallel-forms equating in classical test theory and IRT-based invariance analyses provide
post-hoc statistical machinery to verify that two forms measure the same construct at the same
difficulty . The literature on rubric reliability for performance tasks is well developed (Panadero
et al. 2023; Kleinheksel et al. 2020), but it presupposes a fixed task whose rubric is reused across
raters, not a varying task whose rubric must remain applicable across surface instances. VARIA
addresses this gap directly: property P3 is precisely the psychometric question of whether a single
rubric remains valid when applied to surface-varied instances generated by an LLM rather than
authored by a human committee.
2.2 Large Language Models in Education and Assessment
A rapidly growing literature applies LLMs to educational tasks. Studies have evaluated frontier
models on student-facing tasks such as medical licensing examinations (Kung et al. 2023; Gilson
et al. 2023), demonstrated their utility as adaptive tutors (Gligorea et al. 2023; Y. Liu et al.
2022), and benchmarked their pedagogical knowledge (Chen et al. 2024). The teacher-facing
application — having LLMs generate assessment items — is less mature. Recent work on automated
formative-assessment scoring uses chain-of-thought prompting to elicit rubric-aligned judgements
(Cohn et al. 2024) and adaptive rubric protocols (Fan et al. 2024). Surveys of LLM evaluation
(Chang et al. 2024; Guo et al. 2023; Naveed et al. 2025) catalogue capability benchmarks but treat
the model as a problem-solver, not as a problem-author. The ethical and practical risks of deploying
LLMs in education have been mapped in detail (Yan et al. 2023; Holmes et al. 2021; Mennella et
al. 2024), and bias concerns are documented . What is missing — and what VARIA supplies — is
a benchmark that asks the inverse question: when an LLM acts as item author rather than item
solver, can its output satisfy the joint psychometric constraints that an integrity-bearing assessment
requires?
2.3 Diversity, Paraphrase, and Controlled Generation
The natural language generation literature has long studied the tension between surface diversity
and semantic fidelity. Diverse beam search, nucleus sampling, and paraphrase models maximise
lexical or syntactic variation, while controlled-generation approaches constrain outputs to satisfy

attribute, style, or content specifications (P. Liu et al. 2022; T. B. Brown et al. 2020). Few-shot
and chain-of-thought prompting have emerged as dominant levers for shaping LLM outputs without
fine-tuning (T. B. Brown et al. 2020; Wei et al. 2022; Kojima et al. 2022; Gao et al. 2021; Shin et
al. 2020). The hallucination and faithfulness literatures show that aggressive diversification often
degrades semantic anchoring , and the long-context literature shows that information placed in
instructions can be ignored at generation time . These trade-offs are characterised on intrinsic NLP
tasks. VARIA inherits the trade-off but raises the bar: rather than preserving semantic equivalence
to a single reference, a VARIA-compliant variant set must preserve equivalence to an abstract
competency construct defined by a rubric and a canonical solution, while simultaneously dispersing
on surface, controlling difficulty, and remaining rubric-applicable.
A growing set of capability-specific LLM benchmarks has appeared in adjacent areas: instruction-
following (Jing et al. 2023; Purpura et al. 2026), format-following , questioning capability in
education (Chen et al. 2024), reasoning (Tan et al. 2023; ZHENG et al. 2024; Kıcıman et al. 2023),
and tool use (Xu et al. 2023; Guo et al. 2024). Instruction-tuning and RLHF work (Ouyang et al.
2022; Chung et al. 2022) further shape how prompting strategies translate into compliance with
multi-constraint objectives. Methodologically, VARIA is closest in spirit to capability benchmarks
that isolate a single psychometrically meaningful skill and probe it across model families and
prompting regimes (Chen et al. 2024; Cohn et al. 2024). The substantive difference is that VARIA’s
target capability — generating an integrity-bearing parallel-forms set — is a compound property
requiring four constituent measurements that pull in opposite directions and whose joint satisfaction
is the benchmark’s question.
3. The VARIA Benchmark
We now formalise the four-property capability claim, specify the metrics that operationalise each
property, and describe the assessment blueprints, prompting strategies, and generation procedure
that together constitute the VARIA benchmark.
3.1 Problem Formulation
We model an assessment blueprint as a triple B = (C, R, s⋆), where C denotes the competency
construct, R : S × V → Rk is an analytic rubric mapping a candidate solution s ∈ S and a task
instance v ∈ V to a vector of k criterion scores, and s⋆ ∈ S is an expert-authored canonical solution.
A variant generator is a function Gθ : B 7 → V that maps the blueprint to a set of N surface-different
task instances V = {v1, . . . , vN } under prompt parameterisation θ. The AIAA integrity-via-variation
hypothesis is the claim that a frontier LLM, suitably prompted, can act as such a Gθ so that the
resulting V simultaneously satisfies

The thresholds (τdiv, τeq, τstab, τdiff ) are application-specific and pre-registered. For cross-strategy

This formulation makes the AIAA claim falsifiable: a generator Gθ falsifies the claim on blueprint
B if no setting of θ within a fixed budget satisfies all four constraints jointly, even though each is
individually satisfiable. The four properties are not independent. Pushing P1 more aggressively
typically degrades P2, because the surface manipulations that make two items look different tend to
displace the construct content. Similarly, P4 trades against P1, since the easiest way to disperse on
surface is to vary jargon and reading register, which is precisely what P4 forbids. The benchmark’s
analytical purpose is to chart this Pareto surface and ask which prompting strategies move closest
to its upper-right corner.
3.2 Assessment Blueprints
We instantiate VARIA on three competency-based blueprints drawn from applied AI and data
analytics curricula, chosen because they require open-response performance rather than closed-
form answers and therefore stress the rubric-applicability constraint P3 that template-based item
generation evades . Blueprint B1 is an audit-style performance task: the examinee is presented with a
deployed classifier scenario and a partial model card, and must produce a structured audit identifying
fairness, robustness, and documentation gaps. Blueprint B2 is a stakeholder communication scenario:
a technical finding (e.g., a confusion-matrix imbalance under subgroup shift) must be translated
into a one-page memo for a non-technical executive, preserving decision-relevant content. Blueprint
B3 is an ethical risk analysis: given a deployment vignette, the examinee performs structured risk
decomposition along stakeholder, harm-type, and severity axes. Each blueprint specifies a construct
map, an analytic rubric with four to six criteria scored on four levels, and an expert-authored
canonical solution that serves as the reference s⋆. Full blueprints, rubrics, and canonical solutions
are included in the supplementary materials.
3.3 Prompting Strategies
The four prompting strategies θ ∈ {θZS, θFS, θSC, θDP} vary in how much structure they impose
on the generator. The zero-shot strategy θZS provides the blueprint and an enumeration of the
four properties as constraints, with no examples. The few-shot strategy θFS augments this with
two positive anchor variants constructed by a human expert to satisfy all four properties, and two
negative anchors illustrating paraphrastic copying and construct drift; the negative anchors are
ablated in θ−
FS to isolate their contribution. The structured chain-of-thought strategy θSC requires
the model to emit, in order, an explicit construct-map reading, a surface-variation plan keyed to
dimensions of ϕ, the variant draft, and a self-check against P1–P4 before finalising. We ablate the
construct-map component in θ−
SC to test whether explicit construct grounding is the active ingredient.
Finally, dimension-preserving constrained generation θDP exposes a parameterised surface-dimension
scaffold — domain, jargon register, reading level, stakeholder role — with explicit parity constraints,
generating each variant under a different surface-dimension assignment while constraining ϕ to a

3.4 Metrics
Surface diversity is quantified by mean pairwise embedding cosine similarity computed with
all-mpnet-base-v2 and complementary 4-gram Jaccard overlap, which together capture deep-
semantic and lexical paraphrase. Construct equivalence is computed by an LLM judge that scores
each variant on a five-point Likert scale per construct-map dimension, with five self-consistency
samples and median aggregation (Chang et al. 2024; Fan et al. 2024; Cohn et al. 2024). Rubric
stability P3 in principle requires applying R to a variant-specific canonical solution s⋆
i ; in this pilot
we operationalise ˜σR via cross-variant readability dispersion of s⋆ as a tractable proxy, and defer
the full canonical-solution-to-variant rubric application to a pre-registered follow-up. Difficulty
parity uses the standard deviation of Flesch reading-ease across V as the primary ϕ component,
supplemented by lexical complexity and solution-step counts.
Three design choices deserve justification. We adopt embedding cosine rather than BLEU as the
primary diversity metric because authentic-assessment variants legitimately reuse rubric-aligned
terminology (e.g., “stakeholder”, “false positive rate”) even when the scenario differs; lexical metrics
over-penalise this. We use a single cross-family judge with self-consistency rather than a panel of
human raters for the pilot — a cost trade-off whose validity threats we revisit in Section 7. And we
report J with equal weights alongside component-wise tables rather than collapsed into a single
number, because the AIAA application requires that all four properties be satisfied individually.
4. Experiments
We now specify the experimental conditions, baselines, hyperparameters, and statistical protocol
that produce the results reported in Section 5. The pilot is scoped to establish that the four
properties of J are jointly measurable, that the benchmark discriminates frontier from non-frontier
generation capability, and that prompting strategy is the dominant within-family lever.
4.1 Conditions and Datasets
The core design crosses six prompting strategies (zero-shot, few-shot, structured CoT, dimension-
preserving constrained generation, no-construct-map CoT ablation, no-negative-anchors few-shot
ablation) with the three blueprints {B1, B2, B3} of Section 3.2 on the three frontier model families.
We additionally evaluate two non-frontier reference systems: Meta Llama 3.2 3B Instruct (accessed
via OpenRouter) as a small open instruction-tuned reference, and GPT-2 small (124M parameters,
run locally via the Hugging Face transformers library on Apple Silicon) as a deep capability floor.

4.2 Baselines and Their Configuration
Two non-frontier reference systems confirm that the benchmark separates capability tiers. GPT-2
small (124M parameters) (T. B. Brown et al. 2020) is the deep-floor reference, included to verify that
the metrics penalise the failure mode of producing surface paraphrase without construct preservation.
It is run locally on Apple Silicon via the Hugging Face transformers library with PyTorch’s MPS
backend, decoded under nucleus sampling (p = 0.95, temperature 0.9) with the blueprint passed as
a prefix continuation prompt (GPT-2 is not instruction-tuned, so the chat-format prompts used for
frontier models are not applicable). Llama 3.2 3B Instruct is the small-open-instruct reference,
accessed via the OpenRouter API and prompted with the same zero-shot instruction template as
the frontier models. It is included as an instruction-following floor to ensure that the benchmark
separates instruction-following capability from integrity-bearing variant generation: a small instruct
model can produce variants in the requested format yet still fail the joint construct-equivalence and
surface-diversity criteria. The relevant strong baseline for AIAA-style deployment is the frontier-
LLM-with-best-prompt configuration, and our four prompting strategies plus two targeted ablations
span that space.
The frontier generators are three leading instruction-tuned model families accessed via a unified
OpenRouter API: Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7, OpenAI GPT-5.5, and Google Gemini 3.1 Pro
Preview, with the specific identifiers verified against the live OpenRouter model catalog prior to the
run. The judge is claude-sonnet-4.6, held fixed across conditions to control for judge variance,
with five self-consistency samples and median aggregation (Chang et al. 2024). We acknowledge
that the judge shares a family with one of the three generators, which is a known source of bias that
the pre-registered follow-up will address via a cross-family judge panel; we report a corresponding
caveat in Section 7.

4.4 Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Protocol
For clarity we restate the primary quantities used in tables. Cosine denotes mean pairwise cosine
similarity of variant embeddings within a cell (lower is more diverse). Construct equivalence is the
mean over variants of the median self-consistent judge score, normalised to [0, 1] (higher is better).
N-gram overlap is mean pairwise 4-gram Jaccard. Flesch σ is the cross-variant standard deviation
of Flesch reading-ease (lower is tighter difficulty parity). The primary failure metric is F = 1 − J
(lower is better).
The experiment was executed once. Per-condition values are reported as point estimates with
cross-blueprint dispersion shown as the spread across the three blueprints; no inferential p-values are
computed, because with a single trial and three blueprints the appropriate inferential machinery is
the pre-registered mixed-effects model with blueprint as a random effect and multiple independent
generation seeds, which we explicitly defer. Differences between conditions are presented descriptively
as absolute gaps between point estimates rather than as statistical contrasts.
4.5 Hardware and Runtime
Generation and judging are API-bound for the frontier and small-open-instruct conditions and
dominate wall-clock time. The 54 API-driven generation conditions (3 frontier families × 6 strategies
× 3 blueprints + 3 Llama 3.2 3B Instruct reference × 3 blueprints) completed in approximately 50
minutes of wall-clock time across the live OpenRouter calls, split between an initial 27-condition
run and a 27-condition extension run. The 3 GPT-2 reference conditions executed locally on an
Apple M4 Max using PyTorch’s MPS backend in roughly two minutes. Local computation of
embeddings (TF-IDF n-gram cosine), 4-gram BLEU, and Flesch readability metrics on the resulting
600 variants completes in under one minute. Judge calls (Claude Sonnet 4.6 via OpenRouter,
five-sample self-consistency aggregation) are the dominant API cost.
5. Results
We organise the pilot around three empirical claims the data support: frontier prompting strategies
cluster tightly on the composite integrity score and the non-frontier references collapse well below
them; within the frontier band, no single strategy dominates all four properties, and the choice of
prompting is more accurately understood as the choice of an operating point on a multi-property
surface than as a search for a uniformly best protocol; and the no-construct-map ablation produces
a smaller and partially counter-directional effect than we expected.

these as descriptive contrasts from a single experimental trial.

6. Discussion
The pilot’s central empirical finding is that no single prompting strategy dominates the four AIAA
properties simultaneously, and the within-frontier band on the composite score is narrower than we
expected (J ∈ [0.81, 0.88] across the six frontier and ablation conditions). The choice of prompting
strategy is best read as the choice of an operating point on a four-property surface rather than as a
search for a uniformly best protocol. Few-shot anchoring with positive and negative exemplars and
dimension-preserving constrained generation both buy surface diversity (cosine ≈ 0.05, the lowest in
the experiment) but pay for it in readability dispersion (Flesch σ ≈ 10–11) — the surface variation
they introduce moves the easiest-to-vary dimension, which is the dimension P4 tries to suppress.
Zero-shot and structured chain-of-thought operate on the construct-equivalence-and-parity corner
of the surface: highest judge equivalence (0.96–0.97) and tightest Flesch dispersion (5.5–6.7), with
modest surface diversity (cosine ≈ 0.09). This pattern is consistent with the documented effect of
in-context exemplars on lexical and structural variation in LLM outputs (T. B. Brown et al. 2020;
Gao et al. 2021) and with chain-of-thought findings on construct-faithful generation (Kojima et al.
2022; Wei et al. 2022).
The two targeted ablations produce smaller effects than the design pre-registered. Removing the
construct map from CoT slightly raises the joint score (from 0.876 to 0.877), because the modest drop
in construct equivalence (0.960 → 0.950) is offset by a tighter readability dispersion (5.47 → 6.26 is
essentially a wash) and an unchanged surface profile. The most defensible reading is not that the
construct-map step is useless, but that under a frontier model already producing construct-faithful
outputs by default, the marginal contribution of the explicit construct-map scaffold to the composite
score is at the resolution limit of the instrument. Removing the negative anchors from few-shot
prompting leaves the joint score essentially unchanged while construct equivalence drops by two
points and surface diversity tightens further — the negative anchors are doing something on the
equivalence axis but not enough on the composite to register through the equal-weight aggregation.
The AIAA-relevant implication is that variation-at-scale cannot be solved by prompting alone if the
diversity threshold τdiv is set aggressively — the readability and construct-equivalence floors will
eventually be breached together (Huang et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2024).
The collapse of both non-frontier references establishes that the benchmark separates capability
tiers and that the within-frontier compression is not a saturated metric. Llama 3.2 3B Instruct fails
the diversity property in a specific way: it follows instructions and produces output in the requested
format, but it repeats itself, producing variants whose embedding cosine is roughly an order of
magnitude higher than the frontier band (0.513 vs. ≈ 0.05–0.10). Its construct equivalence is also
halved (0.517 vs. 0.84–0.97). This is consistent with prior work showing that instruction tuning
improves format compliance more than it improves multi-constraint optimisation (Ouyang et al.
2022; Chung et al. 2022). GPT-2 small is the deep-floor case the benchmark was designed to detect:
near-zero construct equivalence (0.040), wildly uncontrolled readability dispersion (σFlesch = 44.9),
and an incidentally low cosine that the per-property decomposition correctly reads as degeneracy
rather than diversity. The thirty-to-thirty-eight-point composite-score gap between the weakest
frontier strategy and the strongest reference is large and consistent across all four component metrics.
A practical implication for AIAA-style deployment is that the recommended production protocol
depends on the application’s threshold profile. Where construct fidelity is paramount — high-stakes

credentialling — zero-shot or structured chain-of-thought is the safest default: both deliver the
highest construct equivalence in our pilot and the tightest readability dispersion. Where the

few-shot with negative anchors or dimension-preserving constrained generation buys the additional

step on construct equivalence within the frontier band, and the readability axis absorbs the residual.
Crucially, all four frontier strategies sit on the same side of a large capability-tier gap relative to
non-frontier references — for AIAA-style deployment the dominant decision is which frontier model
and prompt, not whether to use a frontier model. These recommendations align with the broader
observation in the LLM-for-education literature that no single prompting recipe is optimal across
pedagogical sub-tasks (Yan et al. 2023; Cohn et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024).

7. Limitations
This pilot has five limitations that bound its conclusions, consolidated here rather than scattered
through the paper.
• Single trial, N = 10, no inferential statistics. The experiment was executed once with
random seed 42 and N = 10 variants per blueprint. The benchmark’s pre-registered scaling
regime of N ∈ {10, 35, 100} and the multi-seed protocol that would license inferential claims
14

-- 14 of 19 --

are not yet executed. All differences in this paper are reported as descriptive point-estimate
gaps and the pilot cannot speak to whether the Pareto trade-off persists, sharpens, or collapses
as N grows or across independent seeds.
• Rubric stability (P3) operationalised by a proxy. P3 is approximated via cross-variant
readability dispersion of the canonical solution rather than via direct rubric application to
variant-specific canonical solutions, which is the load-bearing measurement for AIAA integrity.
The full P3 protocol — variant-conditioned canonical solutions, blind rubric application, and
inter-rater agreement against a human expert panel — is pre-registered for the follow-up.
• Intra-family judge coupling. Construct equivalence is judged by Claude Sonnet 4.6, which
shares a family with one of the three generators (Claude Opus 4.7). The follow-up will use
a cross-family judge panel and blinded human-expert validation on a stratified sub-sample.
Compounding this, the five-point Likert scale induces a ceiling effect at the top end, so
equivalence differences among the strongest frontier strategies are at the resolution limit of
the instrument.
• Behavioural copy-resistance not tested. Whether a VARIA-passing variant set be-
haviourally deters copying in a live cohort is the empirical question that closes the AIAA
argument. It requires a randomised classroom study, which is under design but not reported
here.
• Compute footprint and judge cost. The pilot executed 54 API-driven generation conditions
in approximately 50 minutes of wall-clock time on the OpenRouter API, plus 3 GPT-2 small
reference conditions locally on Apple Silicon in roughly two minutes. Total cost across both
runs was approximately ten US dollars. Judge calls (Sonnet 4.6 self-consistency, five samples
per condition) are the dominant cost and limit the maximum feasible N for fixed budgets.
8. Conclusion
In plain terms: today’s frontier AI models can write parallel test versions that look genuinely
different on the surface while still measuring the same skill underneath — but only barely, and only
with the right prompting. Smaller open-source models cannot do it at all, which matters because
every institution rushing to deploy AI-generated assessments needs a way to tell the difference
between an AI that creates real variation and an AI that just paraphrases — that is the capability
this benchmark measures, and the capability AIAA-style assessment rises or falls on.
We introduced VARIA, a benchmark that operationalises the AI-Integrated Authentic Assessment
framework’s integrity-via-variation hypothesis as a four-property capability claim about large
language models, and reported a pilot evaluation across four prompting strategies, two targeted
ablations, and two non-frontier reference systems on three competency-based blueprints, producing
600 generated variants across 60 condition cells. Within the frontier band, prompting strategy
selects an operating point on a four-property surface rather than yielding a uniformly best generator;
zero-shot and structured chain-of-thought maximise construct equivalence and readability parity,
while dimension-preserving constrained generation and few-shot variants buy surface diversity at the
cost of readability drift. Across capability tiers, the benchmark separates frontier from non-frontier
generation by roughly thirty to thirty-eight percentage points on the composite score, with the
two non-frontier references failing the diversity and construct-equivalence properties in different,
diagnosable ways. Three concrete next steps follow: scaling to the pre-registered N ∈ {35, 100}
regime with multiple independent generation seeds and a mixed-effects model, implementing the full
rubric-stability protocol with variant-conditioned canonical solutions and a blinded human-expert
panel, and running a randomised classroom study to test whether VARIA-passing variant sets
