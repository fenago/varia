How VARIA fits

VARIA is not just one more authentic assessment. It is the trust layer the cohort's own framing requires and nobody else in the cohort is likely to bring, and it now runs all the way to a hire.

Application over recall.Every VARIA blueprint is a performance task with an analytic rubric. There is no recall item type in the system.
How over what.Rubric criteria score process (evidence, prioritisation, reasoning under shift), and each student's adapted model answer lets faculty grade the reasoning path, not a fixed key.
Employer-valued.Employer partners contribute real problem briefs. One brief becomes a different version per student, so every student does a slice of that employer's work.
Evidence all parties can trust.Four integrity checks gate every release and the compliance console records why each set went out. When assessments are personalised and AI-generated, every partner will be asked "how do you know two students got an equivalent task?" VARIA is the only answer in the room with a benchmark behind it.
Portable signal.A graded task becomes a signed work sample: the task, the rubric result, the skills, the integrity proof and the employer's validation, exported as an Open Badges 3.0 credential with a verify link.
Challenge → version → work sample → portfolio → talent view → outcome.Students choose which employers see which samples. Employers read the work, endorse it against their own bar, and log interviews, offers, hires and ramp time where they happen.

Honest gaps to name before someone else does

  • Records live in one browser until a student shares them. Institution-wide numbers need a durable, college-held record store. That decision is open.
  • Work samples are signed with a demonstration key. Production needs a college-held key and a public verify endpoint.
  • Students and employer reviewers are not authenticated. Reviewer identity is a work email; the student share page stands in for a student login.
  • No learner-facing delivery inside the LMS. Students receive tasks through Canvas; Canvas integration is still a disabled button.
  • Rubric stability (P3) is a proxy. The full protocol is pre-registered, not built.
  • Live generation at class scale has been tested in demo mode only.

What the app now measures natively

  • Rubric scores by criterion, score spread by version, difficulty appeals (student mastery and fairness).
  • Integrity report per set, thresholds, audit trail (institution).
  • Blueprints validated by employers, partners adopting evidence records, adoption observed through verification events, and the five-question employer satisfaction survey (the three required employer rows).
  • Endorsements, interviews, offers, hires and hours-to-productive, logged against the record (the optional employer rows, collected where they happen).
  • Skills per work sample, mapped from rubric criteria to employer and O*NET-style tags; portfolios per learner ID.

Sep 2026 Due
Finalise assessment concepts; begin prototype design and development
  • We are ahead of the milestone: the prototype is deployed, and it already covers instructor, institution, employer and student roles end to end.

Oct 2026 Due
Finalise employer validation approach
  • The approach is built, not proposed: an employer contributes a problem brief, then validates the rubric and scenario bank once through a self-contained review link, signs off with a work email, and answers the five-question survey. Every version inherits the validation.
  • Show the review page live; it needs no account and stores nothing anywhere but the reviewer's browser until they send the result back.
  • Ask whether the cohort wants a shared sign-off form; ours can be exported as JSON if so.
Nov 2026 Due
Conduct employer validation checkpoint
  • Run one partner through the real flow: contribute a challenge, review three sample versions, edit the scenario bank, sign off, complete the survey. The validated percentage and satisfaction score come out of the console the same day.
  • Then hand the same partner the talent view with one shared work sample and ask them to endorse it. That is the first observed-adoption event.
Dec 2026 Finalise v1 prototype with technical integration documentation and one employer checkpoint output
  • Integration is a strength: static site, no server, no institutional data on any server, faculty bring their own AI key. Nothing for MDC IT to host.
  • Artifacts to submit: the architecture spec, the deployment README, an integrity report CSV, a validation record, and an Open Badges 3.0 credential with its verify link.
  • Decide the two structural items before pilot: a durable record store for partners, validations and evidence records, and a college-held signing key. Name the Canvas gap and the plan: per-student export in December, LTI or Canvas API in the pilot cycle if the cohort wants it.
Q1–Q2 2027 · Pre-pilot, pilot, evaluation
Jan 2027 Pre-pilot feedback session: soft launch, faculty demo or employer demo
  • Offer to be the cohort's demo. The demo workspace needs no key: one student's path from an employer challenge to an offer is on the home page, with every panel clickable.
  • Ask peers to try Import with their own assignment sheet, and to send a review link to one of their own employer contacts.
Feb 2027 Finalise prototype: onboard faculty, configure courses, complete QA
  • Onboarding is the Getting started page. Target: a colleague goes from upload to released set in under twenty minutes without training.
  • QA gate: live generation on all three blueprints with real keys and a real roster; every set clears P1, P2, P4; two partners validated; records signed with the college key if it exists by then.
  • Record the threshold profile on the compliance console before pilot.
Mar 2027 Due
Begin pilot
  • Two sections, one receiving variants and one the fixed task, same rubric. That yields the persistence-correlation and mastery comparisons the framework asks for.
  • Students in the variant section get a portfolio and choose whether to share with the partner employers. Record the baseline: prior-term scores on the same assignment.
Apr 2027 Continue pilot; at least one student and one employer validation checkpoint
  • Student checkpoint: the "not a test" rate, self-efficacy, and two questions on perceived fairness of their version. Difficulty appeals are a built-in metric; zero on a released set is a headline.
  • Employer checkpoint: partners endorse a stratified sample of shared work in the talent view and log any interviews. Endorsement rate and observed adoption come out of the console.
May 2027 Close pilot; collect data; begin evaluating student outcomes
  • Native data: rubric scores by criterion and version, integrity reports, validation records, satisfaction, endorsements, outcome events, verification events, audit trail.
  • Collected outside: engagement, time on task, persistence, satisfaction with the course.
  • The paper's pre-registered follow-up (rubric stability, copy resistance) can be the evaluation design, turning the pilot into a publishable second study.
Jun 2027 Due
Submit all final artifacts from validation loops, outcomes and evaluations
  • Artifacts: blueprints, released variant sets, integrity reports, employer validation records, Open Badges credentials, the outcome log keyed by learner ID, the evaluation write-up, and the app itself as an open asset.

  • Propose that the cohort's shared rubric (faculty practice outcome, validated across 3+ institutions) be authored as a VARIA blueprint, so validation, variation and evidence records share one format.

  • Scale story: a static site any institution deploys in minutes, blueprints as portable files, and evidence records as Open Badges 3.0 credentials that travel with the learner. That is the "15+ institutions using shared assets" metric with a concrete path.

Employers and industry data
Name the partners already tied to the course. Each contributes one problem brief that feeds the scenario bank, validates one rubric, and gets a talent view of the students who share their work.
Technical constraints
None on hosting. Constraints are API cost per run, faculty holding their own AI keys, records living in the browser until a record store is chosen, and no LMS integration yet.
Unresolved, needs support
Canvas delivery of per-student tasks; a durable, college-held record store; a college signing key for evidence records; institutional guidance on AI API use with student-identifying rosters and on students sharing graded work with employers.
Biggest risk to a March 2027 live pilot
Getting personalised tasks into students' hands inside the LMS with grades flowing back, and the record store decision. Mitigation: a December decision on both, and IT sign-off secured by January.

One strategic note

The cohort framework assumes AI-enhanced assessments are trustworthy. The paper shows that assumption is conditional, and VARIA is the instrument that makes it checkable. Position MDC's contribution as the cohort's integrity infrastructure and its employer bridge, not only its pilot: the one place where an employer's problem, a student's verified work, and a hire are connected by a record all parties can check. That maps to three required outcomes at once, shared assets adoption, employer validation, and a shared rubric across institutions, and supplies the join key for every long-horizon row. We supply the join key, not the outcome.
